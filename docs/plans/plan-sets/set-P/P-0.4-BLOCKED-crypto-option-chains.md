# P-0.4 is `BLOCKED:UPSTREAM` — Deribit's terms forbid it

DATA-005 §11 names S5 as "Crypto options public market data (e.g. Deribit public
API)" and marks it **"Market data endpoints only; confirm US access terms"**. The
terms were confirmed before any code was written, per Set P §5. They forbid it, on
two independent grounds.

Per the Execution Board §5 and Set P §5, the term is quoted, the next candidate is
named, and **nothing was built against it**.

---

## The terms

Both from Deribit's Terms of Service (DRB Panama Inc.),
`https://statics.deribit.com/files/TermsofServiceDeribit.pdf`, extracted from the
PDF text.

### 1. Restricted jurisdictions — Article 2.4

> "2.4. Restricted jurisdictions: The access or use of our Platform and the
> services we offer is not allowed if you are located, incorporated or otherwise
> established in, or a resident of: (i) the United States of America; (ii) the
> Republic of Panama; (iii) any other jurisdiction designated as a restricted area
> on our Website (as amended from time to time); (iv) a jurisdiction where it would
> be illegal for you to access or use our Platform or use our services; or (v) a
> jurisdiction where it would be illegal for us to provide you access to our
> Platform or use our services."

"Platform" is defined in the same document as "the application and underlying
infrastructure made available by us which you can access remotely and where you can
access your Account and trade Products". **There is no carve-out for public
market-data endpoints.** The clause restricts *access or use*, not trading.

This is a stronger bar than the Binance 451 already on record for this project. A
451 is an IP filter, which is a technical fact about one egress address; this is a
written term naming the United States against platform access generally. An
unblocked endpoint would not make it permitted.

### 2. Market data may not be processed — Article 4.6

> "4.6. Use of Market Data: The use of Market Data and/or Derived Data is for
> personal use only. You are not allowed to aggregate, resell, publish, forward or
> in any other way process Market Data and/or Derived Data (except for personal
> use) without explicit approval from us. In case you wish to make use of this
> Market Data and/or Derived Data for any non-personal use, please contact
> info@deribit.com."

"Market Data" is defined to cover data "made available by us via our Platform
(including, without limitation, through the API)" — API-sourced data explicitly.
"Derived Data" covers what is computed from it.

Note the verb **"process"**. P-0.4 would persist chains hourly, compute and store
implied volatility, and build surfaces from them. That is processing, and it is
prohibited without written approval regardless of jurisdiction.

---

## Why this is not worked around

Three tempting moves, each rejected:

- **"Public endpoints aren't the Platform."** Article 2.4 restricts *access or use
  of the Platform and the services we offer*; the API is served by that platform
  and 4.6 names the API explicitly. Reading a carve-out into silence is building
  against terms.
- **"It's personal use — it's one person's research bot."** Possibly true today,
  and it stops being true the moment the platform has a second user or trades
  capital. A data dependency whose legality expires on a business event is not a
  foundation for a forward-only series meant to accrue for years.
- **"The endpoint responds, so it's fine."** Compliance is not measured by HTTP
  status. This is the exact reasoning that makes a 451 feel like the only real
  constraint.

---

## What unblocks it

One of three, in descending order of preference. **All three need a human
decision** — this is a roadmap gate, not an engineering choice.

1. **Written approval from Deribit** for non-personal use of Market Data, via
   `info@deribit.com` as Article 4.6 directs. This resolves 4.6. It does **not**
   resolve 2.4, so it is necessary but probably not sufficient.

2. **A different free, US-permitted venue.** The candidate to evaluate next is
   named below.

3. **Accept that the platform has no crypto option chains** under ADR-0027, and
   record that DA-12's crypto half is unsatisfiable on free data. This is a real
   option and should not be dismissed: it is better than a data dependency the
   platform is not entitled to.

## Next candidate: on-chain options protocols

**Derive (formerly Lyra) v2**, and secondarily **Aevo**. Both publish
option-chain market data — strike, expiry, bid/ask, mark, IV — through public APIs
and subgraphs, and both are decentralised protocols rather than centralised
exchanges with a Panamanian ToS naming the United States.

**Their terms have NOT been verified.** That verification is the first step of
whoever picks this up, and it must happen before any code, on the same standard
applied here: quote the jurisdiction clause and the market-data/redistribution
clause, or mark blocked. The platform already has on-chain plumbing
(`crates/collectors/src/dex`, `dataplane::defi`, `pool_observation`), so the
integration shape is familiar, but that convenience is not a reason to skip the
terms check.

Venues explicitly **not** candidates: Binance (451 on record), OKX and Bybit
(US-restricted), CME/CBOE crypto options (paid data, would need an ADR-0027
reversal per DA-14).

## What was built anyway, and why it was worth it

P-0.4's storage half is done and is not wasted:

- `clickhouse/08_forward_series.sql` defines `option_chain_snapshots` with the
  four-timestamp discipline, `in_liquid_universe` for ADR-P2-26's `universe_id = 1`
  gate, `snapshot_cadence` for the hourly/daily split, and **IV stored with its
  inputs and no greek columns** (INV-07). `AT-08` in `crates/invariants` scans every
  DDL file and fails the build if a greek column is ever added.
- `storage::clickhouse::forward::write_chain_snapshot` is the writer, computing
  `quality_flags` from the row's provenance rather than accepting it as an
  argument.

Both are venue-agnostic. Whoever resolves the terms question writes a parser and a
schedule, not a schema. The same tables serve P-0.5 (Tradier equity chains), whose
blocker is a credential rather than a term.
